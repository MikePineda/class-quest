What actually makes us different?

The idea is not new. Let us look at who is already doing it, then decide what we own.

First, the honest part

This space is crowded

StudyQuest

Upload a PDF, get 40+ game modes. Flappy Bird, Snake, Subway Surfers. Free tier. Closest to us.

StudyFetch

"PDF to Game" plus an AI tutor. 8 to 12 dollars a month.

Knowt

Flashcards, quizzes, matching games. Claims a million users.

GamED.AI

Academic paper, April this year. Multi-agent educational game generation.

We are not first. We should say that before a judge says it for us.

But

They all stop in the same place

  • The game and the content are separate layers. StudyQuest drops your questions into Flappy Bird. Swap the questions, keep the game. Nothing about the game knows what it is teaching.
  • Playing together means a leaderboard. Nobody has a class moving through something together.
  • Nobody proves it works. StudyQuest claims students learn "40% faster". No study, no method, no source.

Our move

We make the AI read the material properly first

What everyone else asks the AI

"Make me 20 quiz questions from this PDF."

You get 20 questions, in whatever order the PDF happened to be in. The AI never worked out what the subject is.

What we ask first

"Read this and tell me what is actually in here."

  needs first    training data, generalisation
  common error   "99% on training means
                  99% on new data"
  level          analyse

That is one entry. A lecture gives us 10 to 40 of them. That list is the map. Not questions, not a game. Just what is in the material, what order it can be learned in, and where people trip.

Put another way

A bad tutor quizzes you on random pages

A good tutor reads the chapter first. Works out that you cannot understand B without A, and that everyone gets stuck on C. Then teaches you.

The map is how we force the AI to be the good tutor.

Themcontent  →  questions  →  game skin
Uscontent  →  map  →  story  →  game

Why the map is worth the extra step

Three things fall out of it

The wrong answers get good

We already asked where students trip, so the wrong options are the real mistakes. Everyone else invents three that sound plausible.

It gets cheap

Map the content once, then build scenes one at a time from the map. Not the whole document every time.

We can prove it

The map says what was supposed to be learned, so it doubles as the test. Nobody else can measure, because nobody else wrote it down.

What comes out

Quest: the story you play through

You commit to an answer before the reveal. The wrong options are the real misconceptions from the map.

Same map, different game

Gauntlet: arcade mode

Same concepts, same art, same engine. Timer and score instead of a story.

This is the proof the map is real. One reading of the document, two different games, no extra work. If the map were fake we could not do this.

The second differentiator

You play it with your class

Concept, not built this weekend. It shows where the product goes.

Look at the bar chart: 52% of the class got that one wrong. That tells a teacher exactly what to reteach. Only possible because the wrong answers came from the map.

Why story plus class, and not something else

The research picked these two for us

The big meta-analysis on gamified learning tested which design features actually change outcomes. Two came out significant: story built into the content, and playing with other people.

Every competitor has neither, or has a leaderboard and calls it social.

Small print for the pitch: those two were significant for behavioural outcomes, not cognitive ones. Worth knowing in case a judge reads the paper.

So, in one line

Everyone else skins a quiz. We understand the material first.

That is the pitch. Not "we made a game generator too".
